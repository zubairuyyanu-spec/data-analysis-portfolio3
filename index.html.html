02 / The approach

Make the reasoning visible.

A useful portfolio explains the decisions behind the result.

01

Ask a focused question

Compare monthly sales volume and revenue. Keep the scope small enough to explain clearly.

02

Check the records

Review dates, missing values, numeric fields, and duplicate month–product pairs.

03

Calculate & compare

Multiply units by price for revenue, then sum by month. Calculate change from the starting value.

04

Communicate honestly

Show the trend with labelled axes. Explain the finding and what the available data cannot establish.

Units growth = (90 − 60) ÷ 60 × 100 = 50%.
Revenue = units sold × unit price, summed across products.

Context matters

A finding is stronger when its limits are clear.

  • These are invented records, not actual market data.
  • Three months cannot establish a long-term trend.
  • Revenue is not profit; costs are not included.
  • The increase does not prove a cause or predict future sales.

Next step: For a real analysis, examine a longer period alongside stock availability, pricing and promotions.

About this sample & how to make it your own

This page is an instructor-created example for a beginner data analysis portfolio. It does not claim personal qualifications, client experience, or completed student work. Add your name and an accurate introduction, reproduce the analysis, and describe your own contribution. Keep the fictional-data disclosure unless you replace the dataset with appropriately sourced data.

Save this file as index.html alongside the files in your sample repository. It uses embedded styles, charts, and data, so it also opens directly in a browser. If you change the data, update the figures, chart data, table and report together.
